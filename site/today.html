2 · Today's energy system

The Sisslerfeld energy system simulated with the weather of the past 365 days and today's settings. A new run is added every month.

What “today” means here

  • A simulation, not measurements and not a forecast. The figures come from the energy-hub model ehubX. They show how a cost-optimal energy system for the area would meet the demand under current conditions. They are not metered data from the plants and networks in the area.
  • Weather of the past year. “Today” uses the hourly weather of the 365 days before the run, up to the end of the previous day (UTC); for this run: the year before the profile date. The data come from the Open-Meteo historical weather archive for one point in the area (47.55° N, 8.01° E); the last few days of the window can be preliminary values.
  • Demand and solar profiles are estimated from that weather. Space heating, space cooling, hot water and building electricity come from machine-learning models (random forests) that use outdoor temperature, hour and weekday; the solar irradiation of each roof orientation from temperature, solar radiation, day of the year and hour. The models are trained on the 2016 demand profiles and the 2018 solar profiles of the model.
  • Industry is not re-estimated. The industrial production profile is the 2016 normalised shape, aligned to the calendar of the weather window. The annual industrial demand stays at the reference level.
  • Everything else uses today's settings. The scenario parameters are at their default values: . Technology costs and energy import prices are fixed model inputs, not current market prices.
  • Model set-up. The whole area is modelled as one energy hub, every hour of the year is simulated (no typical days), and the model minimises the total annual cost knowing the whole year in advance.
  • Updated monthly. On the first day of each month a new run is added, with the weather up to the end of the previous month; earlier “today” runs (names starting with R and the run date) stay in All runs.
